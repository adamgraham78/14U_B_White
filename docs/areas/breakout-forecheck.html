BFC Breakout / Forecheck

One rung, both directions. The same triangle run under pressure and against pressure.

Why this, for this team

Breakout and forecheck are the same picture from opposite ends. Teaching them together means every rep is contested by definition — no unopposed breakouts that fall apart the moment a real forechecker arrives.

The progression

  1. Week 11Forecheck — first man anglesF1 takes the angle (Week 1, applied). F2 reads off F1 and takes away the outlet.
  2. Week 12Breakout — triangle under pressureSupport triangle forms in the D-zone. D-to-D and the wall option.
  3. Week 13Both ways, liveRetrieval under pressure, first-pass options, and the same drill run in both directions.
Drill library is thin here — only 7 tagged drills for 18 blocks. Needs new drills sourced from IHS before Week 11.

What to say on the ice

Same words from every coach. The kids should hear one phrase, not four versions of it.

First man takes the angle, second man takes the outlet.Give him two options before he turns.Skate the puck out or pass it out — don't ring it and hope.Lose it? First man back is the layer.

What goes wrong

  • Both forwards forechecking the same side, outlet wide open.
  • The winger leaving the wall early, so the first pass has nowhere to go.
  • D turning into pressure instead of away from it.
