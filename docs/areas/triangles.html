TRI Triangles

The support shape. Always two options at good angles. Same shape in every zone — only the ice changes.

Blocks
24
Drills
24

Why this, for this team

Breakout, entry and O-zone problems are usually one problem in three jerseys: players don't know where to be relative to the puck and each other. Triangles are the grammar. Every rung above them is this shape run on different ice.

The progression

  1. Weeks 4–7Neutral ice — learn the shapeTwo options at good angles, on the move. Passing is the through-line that connects the triangle.
  2. Weeks 9–14D-zone — breakout supportThe same triangle, formed under forecheck pressure.
  3. Weeks 16–18Entry — the trailerTriangle carried through the neutral zone with a late option behind the puck.
  4. Weeks 22–23O-zone — rotatingTriangles rotating below the dots. This is cycling — it emerges as the last location, not a new system.
Triangles are a through-line, not a rung. They never get retired — they reappear roughly every third or fourth week for the whole season, because everything above them depends on the shape holding.

What to say on the ice

Same words from every coach. The kids should hear one phrase, not four versions of it.

Where's your support?Two options, good angles.Don't stand in his shadow — move to open ice.Pass and go, don't pass and watch.

What goes wrong

  • Puck-watching: three players collapsing to one spot and killing every angle.
  • Supporting behind the puck-carrier's back where no pass exists.
  • Standing still to receive. Feet moving, then the puck.
