1. Contact & Checking CHK

Angling, stick checks, receiving contact, and delivering it. Weeks 1-3 are contact and nothing else.

Why this, for this team

14U is the first year of legal contact, and many players arrive with none of it. Contact is not a unit we bolt on - it is the first thing we teach, before any system, because everything else in this curriculum happens with a body arriving.

The progression

  1. Week 1Positioning & anglingSkate to take ice away. Approach on an angle, stick on the wrong side, close without lunging. No contact required to teach this.
  2. Week 2Stick checks & body positionPoke, lift, press. Get your body between the man and the puck. Box-outs and wall pins.
  3. Week 3Receiving contactTake a hit along the wall with your head up. Absorb into the boards, protective posture, escape pressure. The safety-critical step.
  4. Weeks 4-6Delivering contactLegal check: angle plus timing, hands and elbows in control. And the read of when not to hit.
Weeks 1-3 are checking and nothing else - no triangles, no systems. After Week 6 contact becomes a thread: it lives inside battles, games and rotations, never as a standalone block again. Three refreshers are scheduled at Weeks 10, 14 and 15.

What to say on the ice

Same words from every coach. The kids should hear one phrase, not four versions of it.

Take the ice away, not the body.Head up, shoulder first, into the wall.Stick on the wrong side.Finish it or leave it - don't lunge.

What goes wrong

  • Lunging at the hit instead of skating the angle. Costs the puck and the position.
  • Skating into the boards square and low - the injury posture. Turn the shoulder, keep the head up.
  • Chasing a hit that isn't there while a teammate is left uncovered.
