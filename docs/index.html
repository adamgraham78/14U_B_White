The season, in five things

One hour a week, half ice, twenty-four practices. That is not enough time to be decent at everything, so we are going to be genuinely good at five things and deliberately ignore the rest.

How the year runs

Install tight, then spiral. Nothing gets taught once and abandoned — with absences every week, a concept that appears in only one block is a concept half the team never saw.

Why checking comes first. It is foundational and it is a safety issue. 14U is the first year of legal contact, and many players arrive with none of it. Weeks 1–3 are contact and nothing else — and it turns out angling and box-outs are most of our D-zone system anyway, so we are further ahead in Week 8 than it looks.

Rules for running a practice

  • Segments. Opening game 10 min, Station A ~12, Station B ~12, closing game 15. Two stations; three if a fourth coach shows.
  • One install per practice. Revisits don't count against it. A practice installing breakouts can still revisit triangles in the opening game.
  • Never install in the opening game. It's the late-arrival buffer — a third of the group may miss the first four minutes. Revisit and compete only.
  • The closing game is the week's theme under pressure. Its area always matches the install.
  • Station 3 is bonus only. Shooting, faceoffs, individual skills, when numbers allow. It is not part of the 96 blocks and nothing in this plan depends on it. A kid who misses it misses nothing.
  • Skating and contact are threads. After Week 6 they live inside games and battles. Never a standalone skating or checking block.
  • Position- and number-agnostic. Everything here runs with any number of skaters in any positions. Absences are expected.

What we are not doing

Power play, penalty kill, faceoff plays, set O-zone systems, and dedicated skating or shooting blocks. Not because they don't matter — because 50 minutes a week buys five things done properly or eleven things done badly. Shooting and faceoffs live in the optional Station 3.
